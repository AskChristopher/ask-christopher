## Career path

### Where it started

Christopher's career spans **over two decades in education, instructional design, and learning technology**.

It began in education. He spent approximately **seventeen years teaching**, in both K–12 and higher education.

Throughout his education and career he has been drawn to the intersection of communication, technology, design, and teaching. Those teaching years shaped a conviction that runs through everything since:

> People learn best when they actively build, practice, and solve real problems — rather than simply consuming information.

One constant across his life has been genuine curiosity. He enjoys learning new technologies, exploring new ideas, and understanding how things work. That curiosity has repeatedly opened new career opportunities and continues to guide his professional growth.

### Moving into instructional design

Instructional design was not part of a long-term plan. It developed naturally out of his interest in teaching and technology.

While teaching, Christopher realized he enjoyed *designing* learning experiences as much as delivering them. He found himself thinking about how to organize information, create engaging multimedia, and improve the overall learning experience.

Instructional design let him combine education, visual communication, technology, psychology, and problem-solving into a single profession. It was the natural evolution of what he had already been doing as an educator.

### Prior roles

Before joining Leidos QTC Health, Christopher spent approximately seventeen years teaching across K–12 and higher education.

He taught **graphic design and introductory web design at The Art Institute of California – Inland Empire** for approximately four years, helping students develop both creative and technical skills.

During that same period he also **taught one quarter at California State University, San Bernardino**, and remained involved on that campus for about a year afterward. **The teaching there was one quarter.** The campus involvement that followed is not teaching time, and the two must never be combined into a longer appointment or presented alongside the multi-year posts as an equivalent.

Earlier in his career he taught at **The Art Institute of Las Vegas** for roughly three years, while working nights as a DJ. Balancing both careers taught him discipline, adaptability, and the importance of connecting with people from very different backgrounds.

Teaching across multiple campuses put him in front of students with a wide range of backgrounds and learning styles, reinforcing his belief that effective instruction is less about presenting information and more about helping people build confidence through practice.

He also taught in **K–12 classrooms as a substitute teacher** — approximately ten years in the Inland Empire region and approximately three years in Las Vegas — giving him experience designing instruction for learners across different ages and abilities.

### How to report these durations

> **These posts overlap, and their durations must never be added, subtracted, or ranked beyond what is written above.**

The Las Vegas substitute years ran concurrently with The Art Institute of Las Vegas, and with the DJ nights. The Cal State San Bernardino quarter ran concurrently with Inland Empire.

**The approximately seventeen-year total is a figure Christopher supplied for his teaching career as a whole. It is not a sum of the posts listed above**, and adding those posts together overstates his teaching career by several years. Report each duration as it is written, or report the total as it is written; never compute one from the other, and never offer a total that differs from seventeen.

Comparisons are subject to the same rule. Where the durations above settle which post was longer, saying so is grounded. Ranking posts the durations do not rank — or ranking the campus involvement against a teaching post — is an inference, and it stays wrong even when it happens to be true.

### Expanding into learning technology, automation, and AI

As his career progressed, Christopher became interested not only in how people learn, but in the technology that supports learning and performance.

That curiosity carried him beyond traditional instructional design into digital adoption, learning management systems, workflow automation, enterprise software, Microsoft 365 technologies, and artificial intelligence.

Rather than limiting himself to course development, he became known as someone who enjoys learning new tools and helping others use them effectively — WalkMe, Articulate Storyline, Adobe Photoshop and Illustrator, Microsoft Power Automate, Workday Learning, and emerging AI technologies alike. The consistent interest is in how technology can solve real business problems.

He is now intentionally expanding into AI engineering, studying Python, APIs, Claude Code, the Model Context Protocol (MCP), agentic AI, and modern software development. He views AI as the next evolution of learning technology, and is building practical systems that combine education, automation, and artificial intelligence.

### Turning points

Three moments meaningfully changed the direction of his career.

**Realizing the passion was design, not delivery.** The recognition that what he cared about was not simply teaching, but designing better ways for people to learn.

**Discovering digital adoption technology.** Tools like WalkMe showed him that learning does not have to happen only in a classroom or an eLearning course — it can happen inside the software people use every day.

**Deciding to learn how AI systems are built.** Rather than treating AI as another productivity tool, Christopher chose to understand its construction. That decision led him to publicly document his learning, build projects like Ask Christopher, and begin moving from an AI user to an AI systems builder.

Looking back, the common thread is a commitment to continuous learning. Every major transition — teaching, to instructional design, to learning technologies, and now to AI engineering — began with curiosity and a desire to understand something new.
