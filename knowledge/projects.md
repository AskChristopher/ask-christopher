## How to write an entry

The vision asks the assistant to answer questions like *"Show me your best project"*, *"Why did you build this?"*, and *"What did you learn?"*, and to **connect the projects into one coherent story**. A bare list of repository names cannot do that. Each entry needs the reasoning behind the work.

Use this shape for every project:

```
### Project Name

**Status:** Active | Paused | Complete | Concept
**Repository:** https://github.com/AskChristopher/...

**What it is** — one or two sentences, plain language, no jargon.

**Why it exists** — the problem or question that prompted it. This is the
part visitors actually care about and the part a repo listing cannot supply.

**How it works** — technologies and approach, at the level of detail a
curious peer would want.

**What was learned** — including what went wrong. This is the most valuable
field in the entry and the easiest one to skip.

**How it connects** — its relationship to the other projects and to the
larger arc from AI user to AI systems builder.
```

## Notes for whoever fills this in

- **Only projects that exist.** A planned project described in the present tense is a fabricated credential. Use `**Status:** Concept` and say so plainly, or leave it out.
- Prefer three well-explained projects to nine thin ones. The assistant's answer quality tracks the depth of the entry, not the length of the list.
- Failures and dead ends are worth writing up. They are more instructive than successes and they are what distinguishes a portfolio from a brochure.
- When Phase 3 adds automated GitHub ingestion, this file stays. Generated metadata can supply languages, commits, and file trees; it cannot supply *why you built it*.
